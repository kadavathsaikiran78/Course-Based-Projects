🔬 Random Forest Feature Importance Analysis

Gini Feature Importance Hierarchy

Key Cardiological Insights from Feature Weights:

  1. thal (Thallium Heart Scintigraphy): The strongest predictor. Reversible and fixed perfusion defects correlate heavily with myocardial ischemia.
  2. ca (Major Fluoroscopy Vessels): Visual detection of 1 to 3 blocked coronary arteries provides immediate pathological confirmation of CAD.
  3. oldpeak (ST Depression): Greater depression under exercise stress indicates exercise-induced subendocardial ischemia.
  4. thalach (Max Heart Rate): Inability to achieve normal age-adjusted peak heart rate indicates chronotropic insufficiency.
  5. cp (Chest Pain Type): Asymptomatic CAD presentation or typical angina carry strong differential diagnostic weight.
🏆 Optimal Model Selection Rationale

Selected Random Forest using clinical composite objective (45% ROC-AUC, 35% Recall, 20% F1). Test ROC-AUC: 0.9545, Test Recall (Sensitivity): 0.9286, Test F1-Score: 0.8966, Accuracy: 0.9016.

Clinical Sensitivity Prioritization: In cardiac screening applications, a False Negative (failing to detect an individual with CAD) can lead to unmonitored cardiovascular events, whereas a False Positive is safely resolved by follow-up clinical angiography. Hence, model selection weighted ROC-AUC (45%) and Recall/Sensitivity (35%) above raw accuracy alone.

📖 Project Methodology, Architecture & Academic Reference

AI-Based Early Prediction of Heart Disease Using Clinical Data Analytics

1. Problem Statement & Clinical Context

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading cause of mortality globally, accounting for an estimated 17.9 million deaths annually. Early identification of individuals at high risk of coronary artery disease (CAD) allows for timely lifestyle modifications, pharmacological therapies, and preventive interventions.

This system implements an end-to-end Machine Learning pipeline utilizing clinical parameters (ECG indicators, hemodynamic measurements, metabolic markers, and demographic factors) to assist clinical decision-making.
